# My Professional Journey

I started my career back in 2002 as a Support Engineer at a small ISP in Odesa, Ukraine, while studying 
at Odesa State Polytechnic University.

In 2003, I got my hands on my first database — the heart of a telecom's billing system. That was my first 
experience managing something that was actually critical to the business.

In 2005, I started dealing with the challenge of regularly refreshing multiple development and QA environments 
for a large team of R&D and QA engineers. At some point, I began thinking about how to make this process 
more efficient and scalable. That's when we started exploring the idea of cloud computing and eventually 
built our own private cloud using VMware and HP hardware.

In 2010, I started working with several startups. They brought me their applications, and I made sure they were up and running on the internet. I was responsible for pretty much everything: hosting, domains, DNS, TLS certificates, and application infrastructure. Mostly Nginx, PHP, Node.js, and MySQL.

In 2016, I worked on migrating Google Nest's development infrastructure from AWS to Google Cloud.

In 2018, I made my first move to containers and Kubernetes. That opened another chapter in my 
infrastructure engineering journey.

In 2020, I moved to the Netherlands and started working with FinTech companies. 
Alongside DevOps and SRE, I got more involved in security, helping teams build security practices 
into their infrastructure and development processes.

In 2023, I decided to try something different and took on a Principal Engineer role. 
I designed and built the infrastructure and DevOps foundation for a multi-tenant, multi-environment banking 
integration platform. We started with a PoC and grew it into a production platform that achieved SOC 2 certification.

Looking back, I've worked through quite a few generations of infrastructure — from managing Linux servers and databases
to private clouds, public clouds, containers, Kubernetes, and modern platform engineering.

I've always enjoyed getting my hands dirty, figuring out how things work, solving complicated problems, 
and building things that make life easier for engineering teams. And I don't think that's going to change 
anytime soon.
